I used to think software architecture was mostly about people drawing boxes on whiteboards and deciding which technologies and patterns to use.

But I'm learning that there's so much more to it. Talking to engineers. Listening to their problems. Asking questions. Organising meetings to bring the right people together.

Helping teams learn from each other. Finding problems that nobody owns. Making sure people understand why certain decisions matter.

Sometimes it's about challenging decisions. Other times it's about helping people make their own. And sometimes the most useful thing you can do is just listen.

With AI accelerating software delivery, I think all of this becomes even more important. When teams can build faster than ever, making sure they're building the right things, in the right direction, matters even more.

Architecture is a leadership role, and it goes far beyond technology.
